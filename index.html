Engineering model

Steady, incompressible flow between suction and discharge reference stations. The pump adds mechanical energy to overcome pressure difference, elevation difference, kinetic-energy difference, and system losses.

H = (Pd − Ps)/(ρg) + (zd − zs) + (Vd² − Vs²)/(2g) + hL
Ph = ρgQH   ·   Pb = Ph/ηp   ·   Pe = Pb/ηm

Pressure inputs are gauge pressures. Only pressure differences enter the head balance. Losses represent nonnegative dissipation for forward flow. This is a duty-point calculator, not a manufacturer pump-curve selection tool.

Engineering interpretation

Assumptions and limitations

Steady, single-phase, incompressible flow; constant density, viscosity, efficiencies and gravitational acceleration. The system is represented by two reference stations. Darcy mode assumes circular pipes with uniform diameters within each section, specified lengths, roughness and aggregate minor-loss coefficients. Suction and discharge velocities are based on the respective pipe diameters.

For a pump drawing from and delivering into large reservoirs, station pressures, elevations, and velocities must refer to the reservoir free surfaces, not the pump flange locations. Never mix reservoir station elevations with pump-flange pressure readings without accounting for intervening pipe losses. No NPSH, cavitation, transient water hammer, temperature effects, non-Newtonian behavior, pump performance curve, motor service factor, or efficiency variation with flow is modeled. A positive head result is required for meaningful pumping power.

The fixed-loss mode is appropriate for reproducing an existing design head-loss estimate, not for predicting friction changes with flow. Darcy mode is recommended for flow sweeps.
